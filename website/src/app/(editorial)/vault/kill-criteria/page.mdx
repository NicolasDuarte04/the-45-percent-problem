This page describes the criterion that fired the warning, the
inequality it encodes, the two SE readings that produce different
gap numbers from the same locked data and what each of them actually
divides by, the two stages at which the
criterion is evaluated, and what the firing has and has not changed
about the project's operations. The procedural argument for why a
pre-registered stopping rule is necessary at all sits at the bottom;
the substantive event the rule produced sits at the top.

## The mathematical statement

The kill criterion is a two-condition gate. Both conditions must hold
for M★ to remain the live trading model. Failure on either condition
fires the criterion.

### The two-condition gate

The first condition is that M★ has the lowest mean cross-validation
log-loss across the candidate set <InlineMath math="\{M_0, M_1, M_2, M_3\}" />.
The second condition is that M★'s log-loss must beat M0's by at least
2 standard errors of the difference.

The two-condition structure is load-bearing. Without the first
condition, the project could re-label any model as M★ retroactively.
Without the second condition, the project could promote a model that
beats M0 by a hair on sampling variance and call the result an edge.
The pre-registration sealed both conditions before the tournament
began.

### The decision inequality

Let <InlineMath math="d_i = \mathcal{L}^{M_0}_i - \mathcal{L}^{M^{\star}}_i" />
be the per-match log-loss difference (positive <InlineMath math="d" />
means M★ is better than M0). The criterion requires:

<BlockMath math="\overline{d} \;\geq\; 2 \cdot \mathrm{SE}\!\big(\overline{d}\big)" />

The criterion fires when this inequality fails:

<BlockMath math="\overline{d} \;<\; 2 \cdot \mathrm{SE}\!\big(\overline{d}\big)" />

The firing condition is "M★ does not beat M0 by 2 SE." This can
happen in two ways. M★ might beat M0 by less than 2 SE, which is what
`evaluation/cv_battery_result.json` reports for Phase 8: a 1.75 SE
gap, falling 0.25 SE short of the bar. M★ might also fail to beat M0
at all, which would be a stronger failure mode. Both fire the
criterion under that reading; both trigger the same pre-registered
consequence (`pivot_paper_framing`). A second published figure, 6.22
SE, clears the bar. Neither of those two figures is the paired
<InlineMath math="\mathrm{SE}(\overline{d})" /> written above; what
each one actually divides by, and what the paired construction returns,
is set out in "Two SE readings" below.

The threshold value is 2.0 standard errors, sealed in
`pre_reg_constants.yaml::kill_criterion.threshold_standard_errors` and
mirrored at `kill.ll_gap_se`. The threshold cannot be modified during
the tournament without an OSF amendment, which is itself a public
artifact.

### Why two standard errors

The choice of 2 SE rather than zero is a deliberate buffer at our
sample size. With 64 World Cup matches plus a small calibration
hold-out, a 1-SE rule fires on roughly 16% of repeated draws of an
honest null hypothesis. The 2-SE bar moves the false-positive rate to
roughly 2.5% one-sided, while still being permissive enough that any
genuine model improvement should clear it.

The 2-SE choice also accounts for the multiple-comparison structure
across the four shadow models. A 1-SE rule applied independently to
each shadow candidate would inflate the family-wise false-positive rate
above 50%; the 2-SE rule, combined with the Bonferroni correction on
the Diebold-Mariano comparisons described in
[Evaluation](/vault/evaluation), keeps the family-wise rate below 5%.

<MarginNote>
  An earlier draft of this page used a no-margin rule (any positive
  improvement over M0 was deemed sufficient). That draft predated
  Phase 8 and did not match the actual locked criterion in
  `pre_reg_constants.yaml`. The 2-SE rule is the rule that fired.
</MarginNote>

## Two SE readings

<MarginNote>
  Corrected by [amendment
  v1.2](https://github.com/NicolasDuarte04/the-45-percent-problem/blob/main/osf/amendments/amendment_v1.2_evaluation_reporting_corrections.md)
  on 2026-09-07. Until v1.2 this section called 6.22 a "marginal SE"
  and 1.75 a "paired-difference SE", and explained the gap between
  them as a marginal-versus-paired distinction. That explanation was
  wrong. Neither number is a paired difference. No sealed statistic
  changed; the labels and the reasoning did.
</MarginNote>

Two SE readings of the M2-versus-M0 gap have been published by this
project, 6.22 and 1.75. They differ for two reasons, and neither
reason is the one this page used to give.

They come from **different cross-validation batteries**, with
different folds and therefore different gaps. And each divides its gap
by a **different dispersion of M2 alone** — one by a standard
deviation, the other by a standard error. Neither uses M0's variance.
Neither uses the fold-level pairing. Neither is
<InlineMath math="\mathrm{SE}(\overline{d})" />.

### 6.22: the gap over M2's between-fold standard deviation

`data/calibration/cv_battery_results.json` is the Phase 4
model-development battery, whose folds are five fixed tournament
windows. It reports
<InlineMath math="\sigma_{CV} = 0.006587" /> for M2, computed at
`models/model_registry.py:822` as `np.std(valid_folds, ddof=1)` — the
standard deviation *between* M2's fold losses, with no division by
<InlineMath math="\sqrt{n}" />. Dividing the locked
<InlineMath math="\Delta_{CV} = -0.04096" /> by it yields
<InlineMath math="6.22" />. It answers: how many between-fold standard
deviations of M2's own loss does the gap span?

Two things about this battery matter and are documented in [working
paper §6.4](/vault/models). It ran on four folds, not five — the 2016
fold is empty in this corpus and serialises as `null`. And its fifth
fold *is* the 2022 hold-out, so
<InlineMath math="\Delta_{CV}" /> is not computed on a sample
disjoint from the hold-out verification.

### 1.75: the gap over the standard error of M2's cross-fold mean

`evaluation/cv_battery_result.json` is the Phase 8 pre-registration
battery, whose folds are a stratified five-fold partition of the
calibration matches only, with the 2022 hold-out excluded and scored
separately. It reports
<InlineMath math="\mathrm{m\_star\_vs\_m0\_gap\_se} = 1.7518" />. The
computation is at `src/calibration/run_cv_battery.py:616-619`:

<BlockMath math="\frac{\overline{\ell}_{M_0} - \overline{\ell}_{M_2}}{\mathrm{se\_log\_loss}_{M_2}} = \frac{0.031685}{0.018087} = 1.7518" />

where <InlineMath math="\mathrm{se\_log\_loss}_{M_2}" /> is
<InlineMath math="\mathrm{sd}/\sqrt{5}" /> over M2's own folds. The
variable in the source is literally named `se_for_gate = champ_se` —
the champion's standard error, not the standard error of a difference.
This file carries `sanity_gate_passed: false` and a
`decision_narrative` containing "WARNING: sanity gate NOT passed".

### What the paired construction actually returns

The paired reading is the one the criterion above defines and the one
`evaluation/accuracy_metrics.check_kill_criterion` implements:
<InlineMath math="d_i = \ell^{M_2}_i - \ell^{M_0}_i" />, then
<InlineMath math="\mathrm{se} = \mathrm{sd}(d,\,\mathrm{ddof}{=}1)/\sqrt{n}" />.
Computed on the folds of each battery:

| Battery | n | gap | gap / between-fold SD | gap / mean SE | paired |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 4 (`cv_battery_results.json`) | 4 | 0.040960 | **6.22** | 12.44 | 1.96 |
| Phase 8 (`cv_battery_result.json`) | 5 | 0.031685 | 0.78 | **1.75** | 2.24 |

Three readings follow from this table.

The published pair is not a marginal-versus-paired contrast. It is a
between-fold-SD reading of one battery beside a mean-SE reading of a
different one.

Most of the distance between 6.22 and 1.75 is neither the
<InlineMath math="\sqrt{n}" /> nor the pairing. It is the fold
construction. M2's between-fold spread is 0.006587 in the Phase 4
tournament windows and 0.040443 in the Phase 8 stratified folds, a
factor of 6.1, because tournament-window folds make M2's losses far
more homogeneous than a stratified partition does.

And the honest number is neither 6.22 nor 1.75. On the paired
construction the gap reads 1.96 on the Phase 4 folds and 2.24 on the
Phase 8 folds. It straddles the 2.0 bar. It is not comfortably above
it, as 6.22 suggests. It is not clearly below it, as 1.75 suggests.

### How to read the two together

Both files remain sealed and neither is edited. What changed on
2026-09-07 is the description, and the direction of the correction is
worth stating plainly: it does not rescue the champion. The gate's
verdict was recorded on the wrong denominator, and the corrected
denominator puts the gap on the threshold rather than clear of it.

The pre-registration seals the threshold (2.0) and the consequence
(`pivot_paper_framing`). It does not seal an SE construction — a gap
in the registration that this page previously papered over by
presenting two conventions as though the protocol had chosen between
them. The only construction the project implements against that
constant is the paired one, so that is the one the corrected reading
should carry.

## The two checkpoints

The same 2-SE rule is applied at two stages of the project. The Phase 8
sanity gate runs on cross-validation hold-out data before the
tournament begins. The R16 live checkpoint runs on cumulative
tournament forecasts once the Round of 16 settles. Both stages use the
same machinery; they differ only in which sample of log-losses they
evaluate.

### The Phase 8 sanity gate

The Phase 8 sanity gate ran the kill criterion as a pre-flight check on
the cross-validation hold-out (calibration 2010 to 2021, hold-out 2022
World Cup), before the 2026 tournament started. The full adjudication
table:

| Model | Mean CV LL | Between-fold SD | Δ vs M0 | DM p vs M0 | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M2_fifa** | 0.99337 | 0.00659 | −0.04096 | 0.0032 | Champion (`CHAMPION_LOCKED: true`); sanity-gate warning logged |
| **M3_macro** | 1.02694 | 0.02949 | −0.00739 | 0.3443 | Eligible; below M0 in point estimate |
| **M0_elo** | 1.03433 | 0.03844 | 0.000 | 1.0000 | Baseline |
| **M1_form** | 1.08110 | 0.07514 | +0.04677 | 0.0061 | Disqualified (significantly worse than M0) |

Values are from the locked
`data/calibration/cv_battery_results.json` (Phase 4). M2 cleared the
primary criterion by having the lowest mean log-loss across the
candidate set, with a Diebold-Mariano test against M0 returning
<InlineMath math="p = 0.003" />. The 1.49 SE gap to the runner-up
(M3_macro) that this page previously attributed to the same file is
`ll_gap_in_se` from the *other* battery,
`evaluation/cv_battery_result.json` (Phase 8); amendment v1.2 corrects
the attribution. Note also that the Phase 4 battery evaluated four
folds, not five — the 2016 fold is empty in this corpus and
serialises as `null` — and that its fifth fold is the 2022 hold-out
itself, so this table's means are not computed on a sample disjoint
from the hold-out verification.

The 2-SE sanity gate, as implemented in
`evaluation/cv_battery_result.json`, reports a 1.75 SE gap and
`sanity_gate_passed: false`. The same gap measured against M2's
between-fold standard deviation in the Phase 4 battery is 6.22 SE. On
the paired construction the criterion actually defines, it is 1.96 to
2.24 — see "Two SE readings" above. The pre-registered
consequence (`kill.action: pivot_paper_framing`) took effect: the paper's framing pivoted to the
sanity-gate-warning narrative, and the project committed to
acknowledging the warning transparently in the public ledger and the
vault essays. M★ was not demoted; the pre-registered action is a
framing pivot, not an automatic identity change. The pipeline did not
abort; the engine continues to run, and the forecast log continues to
record every model's probabilities, edges, and divergences.

### The R16 live checkpoint

The same 2-SE rule will be re-evaluated once the Round of 16 settles.
The check fires once, after all eight R16 matches are settled; it is
not re-run weekly thereafter.

The R16 live check is the kill criterion's first contact with live
tournament data. M★ is M2_fifa, sealed in `champion_model.json`. The
comparison is a real M2-versus-M0 head-to-head on live forecasts, not
a degenerate self-comparison. The evaluation happens when the Round of
16 settles, as pre-registered: the statistic is the cumulative
match-level log-loss of M★ (M2) against the M0 baseline over the 72
pre-registered group-stage forecasts, scored against their realized
outcomes, with a paired per-match standard error. The kill criterion
fires if M★ is worse than M0 by at least 2 standard errors on that
statistic.

If the criterion fires, the pre-registered consequence is the same
`pivot_paper_framing` action described for Phase 8: the paper pivots
to the pre-committed contingency framing. The model is not silently
swapped; only an OSF amendment can change M★'s identity mid-tournament
(see the margin note below). Whether or not the criterion fires, the
project publishes the evaluation report within 72 hours of the Round
of 16 settling, following the template committed in the
pre-registration. This live checkpoint is a separate evaluation from
the Phase 8 sanity gate above (the 1.75 SE and 6.22 SE readings on
cross-validation hold-out data); it applies the same 2-SE rule to
live tournament forecasts instead.

The block below is this checkpoint's own live event, a separate
construction from the Phase 8 cross-validation readings above. It is a
paired per-match standard error over the 72 pre-registered group-stage
forecasts re-scored against their realized results, published once when
the Round of 16 settles. Until then it shows the pre-registered interim
sentence; it is never numerically compared to the 1.75 SE or 6.22 SE
cross-validation numbers.

## Operational response

The Phase 8 sanity gate firing triggered a specific, pre-registered set
of changes. The changes are operational rather than computational. The
engine still runs the same way; the claims the project is willing to
make from its outputs have changed.

**What changed:**

1. The paper's framing pivoted from "we built a model that finds market mispricings" to the kill-criterion narrative described in [The 45% Problem](/vault/the-45-percent).
2. The vault essays, this page, and the live ledger acknowledge the sanity-gate warning. The project's brand commitment is to acknowledge the warning visibly, not to bury it inside the locked file.
3. The R16 live checkpoint is treated as a real test of M2 against M0 on live forecasts, not a procedural formality.

**What did not change:**

1. The engine continues to run on its 60-second tick during live tournament hours and 5-minute tick off-hours. The forecast-log API exists, and the frozen champion batch pins the M0 and M2 (M★) per-match distributions the published surfaces score against; per-match logs for the M1 and M3 shadow variants were not committed.
2. The de-vigging machinery, divergence calculation, and [Volatility Gate](/vault/volatility-gate) all remain active. They continue to produce flagged divergences and gate decisions against M★'s (M2_fifa) probabilities.
3. CLV continues to be tracked on the M★ row, measuring whether M2's probabilities lead the market over time. The same metric runs against the four shadow models as pseudo-CLV (see [Evaluation](/vault/evaluation)).
4. The R16 live checkpoint was evaluated on 2026-07-07 when the Round of 16 settled, on a live M2-versus-M0 comparison; it did not fire, with M2 1.47 SE better than M0 on the 72 pre-registered forecasts.

<MarginNote>
  An OSF amendment is the only path to changing M★'s identity during
  the tournament. The amendment, if filed, would be a public artifact
  with its own time-stamped registry record. There is no quiet
  substitution available, and no version of the live site where the
  displayed model can shift between M0, M2, or any other candidate
  without leaving an audit trail.
</MarginNote>

The annotated Git tag `v1.0.0-MSTAR-LOCKED` and the OSF pre-registration
both record the state of the project at the moment of the Phase 8
firing. The OSF record cannot be amended without producing a visible
fork in the audit trail. Together they make the firing, and the
operational response that followed it, permanently verifiable.

## Why pre-registering the stopping rule matters

Pre-registering the kill criterion before the tournament prevents the
worst form of result-chasing: keeping a model alive indefinitely
because no one has formally decided when to stop. The same logic
applies to post-hoc threshold tuning. With the 2 SE bar sealed before
the cross-validation battery ran, there was no path to retroactively
soften the bar to 1.75 SE in order to keep M2 as M★.

Pre-registration also removes the option to quietly remove the
project from public record if the criterion fires. The OSF DOI
`10.17605/OSF.IO/8B5HD` and the annotated Git tag `v1.0.0-MSTAR-LOCKED`
are public and time-stamped. The
Phase 8 sanity-gate warning is now part of that permanent record.
The model cards have been updated with both SE readings; the
terminal displays the warning badge alongside the reading that
clears; the forecast log carries every model's probabilities and
divergences against M★ (M2_fifa) in every row.

<PullQuote cite="this project's pre-registration, OSF 2026-04-22">
  Publishing a null result under a pre-registered stopping rule is not
  failure. It is the project working as designed.
</PullQuote>

The Phase 8 sanity-gate warning is the strongest available
vindication of the pre-registration discipline. Without the 2-SE bar,
the project would have launched a public website with M2 as M★ and
either quietly suppressed the sanity-gate warning that one
of the two locked files surfaces, or reported only the reading
that clears the bar without acknowledging the other.
Pre-registration forces both readings into the public record. With
the bar in place, the project published an honest dual reading on
day one and a clear plan for the R16 checkpoint that follows on live
tournament data. The criterion was not theatre; it bound on the very
first run.
