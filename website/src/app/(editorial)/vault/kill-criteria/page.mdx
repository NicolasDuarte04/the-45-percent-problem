<Dropcap>
  If M★ performs worse than the null baseline by the Round of 16, this
  project publishes a null result, the terminal is frozen, and no further
  forecasts are issued. This is not a contingency plan. It is the pre-
  registered design.
</Dropcap>

The kill criterion exists because a project that can run indefinitely without
consequence is not falsifiable. Pre-registering a stopping rule before the
tournament makes the negative result legible — not as a failure, but as the
correct outcome of an honest test.

<SectionRule />

## The mathematical statement

The kill condition is a one-sided log-loss comparison evaluated at a single
pre-specified checkpoint: the end of the Round of 16 (R16), when at least 16
group-stage matches and the first four R16 matches have settled.

Let <InlineMath math="\mathcal{L}_{M^\star}" /> and <InlineMath math="\mathcal{L}_{M_0}" /> denote the cumulative
mean log-loss of M★ and the null baseline M0 respectively, computed over all
settled matches up to and including R16:

<BlockMath math="\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \log p_i" />

where <InlineMath math="p_i" /> is the model's assigned probability to the realized outcome of
match <InlineMath math="i" />, and <InlineMath math="N" /> is the count of settled matches.

<MarginNote>
  Log-loss (also called log-likelihood loss or cross-entropy) penalizes
  confident wrong predictions more heavily than uncertain ones. A model that
  assigns 0.90 probability to the losing outcome receives a loss of
  <InlineMath math="-\log(0.10) \approx 2.30" /> for that match — far higher than the 0.69
  penalty for an equal-probability guess.
</MarginNote>

The kill criterion fires if and only if:

<BlockMath math="\mathcal{L}_{M^\star}(R16) > \mathcal{L}_{M_0}(R16)" />

That is: M★'s average surprise per match exceeds the null model's. The null
model assigns <InlineMath math="\tfrac{1}{3}" /> to every outcome in the group stage; its
log-loss is a constant <InlineMath math="\log 3 \approx 1.099" /> nats. M★ must beat this number
to remain active.

No margin is required. A single nat of improvement over M0 is sufficient.
The criterion is strict in the statistical sense — it requires *any* positive
improvement — but generous in the practical sense, because the bar is a
deliberately naive comparator.

<SectionRule />

## Why pre-registering the stopping rule matters

Pre-registering the kill condition prevents the worst form of result-chasing:
keeping a model alive indefinitely because no one has formally decided when
to stop.

Without a kill condition, a project that goes negative after 20 matches can
explain away the result ("small sample"), continue to 40 matches, explain
it again, and eventually either find a positive run or simply never publish
the full record. Pre-registration removes that option.

If the criterion fires, we must publish the null result. The ledger remains
visible. The model cards are updated with the outcome. The terminal displays
a permanent banner. There is no path to quietly removing the project from
public record.

<PullQuote cite="this project's pre-registration, OSF 2026-04-22">
  Publishing a null result under a pre-registered stopping rule is not
  failure. It is the project working as designed.
</PullQuote>

## Operational response if the criterion fires

If <InlineMath math="\mathcal{L}_{M^\star}(R16) > \mathcal{L}_{M_0}(R16)" />:

1. The site displays a global banner on every page identifying the null
   result and the matched condition.
2. The divergence terminal is frozen: no new divergence rows are added, and
   the gate status on all existing rows is marked `SUSPENDED`.
3. The ledger continues to accept settled matches for the remainder of the
   tournament — the forecast record remains public — but no new forecasts
   are issued after R16.
4. A null-result report is published under `/vault/null-result` within
   72 hours of the criterion firing, following the template committed in the
   pre-registration.
5. The OSF record is updated with a link to the null-result report.

<MarginNote>
  Steps 2 and 3 are not equivalent. The terminal shows predictive output;
  freezing it is the honest response to a model that has failed its
  pre-registered performance test. The ledger shows historical facts;
  those remain visible regardless of model performance.
</MarginNote>
