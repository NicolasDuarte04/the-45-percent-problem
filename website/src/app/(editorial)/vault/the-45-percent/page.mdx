<Dropcap>
  Bookmakers are good at their job. Across thousands of World Cup matches,
  the markets they set resolve, on average, close to the probabilities those
  markets imply. That is the headline. Read it carefully; it is less
  reassuring than it sounds.
</Dropcap>

The residual — the part of outcome variance the market does **not** explain
— is the subject of this essay. About 45% of it, we will argue, is neither
predictable market edge nor pure randomness. It is something else: a
structural gap between the way tournament markets are priced and the way
tournament outcomes actually fall.

<MarginNote>
  Residual here means variance left over after accounting for market-implied
  probabilities. The 45% figure is not a point estimate of edge; see
  [§ Evaluation](/vault/evaluation) for how it is decomposed.
</MarginNote>

## Why residuals matter more than point predictions

A point prediction says *Brazil wins*. A distribution says *Brazil wins with
probability 0.24, draws 0.28, loses 0.48*. The second statement is harder to
be wrong about, and harder to be right about by accident. It is the only
statement that can be calibrated.

<PullQuote cite="this project, stated plainly">
  The question is not whether a model beats the bookmaker on any single
  match. The question is whether the residuals, aggregated across hundreds
  of matches, are where the model says they will be.
</PullQuote>

## What this project is doing about it

M★ is a bivariate Poisson model with a Dixon-Coles correction, calibrated
on recent international match data and compared nightly to bookmaker-implied
probabilities. Its output is a distribution, not a tip. Its evaluation
metric is calibration, not accuracy.

The project publishes a [Transparency Ledger](/ledger) of every M★
forecast, settled and unsettled, with realized outcomes and the calibration
metrics the pre-registration commits us to.

## What this project is not doing

This is not a tipping service. It does not recommend positions. It does
not claim edge on any single match. It is a public, pre-registered test of
a specific hypothesis about tournament-market residuals — and it has a
[kill-criterion](/vault/kill-criteria) that forces publication of a null
result if the hypothesis fails.
